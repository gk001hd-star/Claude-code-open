## Comprehensive summary

### What the numbers say

Across 876 PubMed-indexed papers spanning 1985–2026, Hans Clevers has **4,673 distinct
co-authors**. Of those, **461 carry an affiliation naming one of the group's three homes**
on at least one shared paper. That 461 is the defensible core of "people who worked in or
with the Clevers group"; the remaining 4,212 are a mixture of genuine pre-2014 lab members
whose addresses PubMed never recorded, and the very large external collaborator network that
comes with consortium papers, mouse-genetics collaborations and organoid biobank consortia.

### Three eras of the group

**1991–2002, immunology.** The group sat in the Department of Immunology at the University
Hospital Utrecht and worked on HMG-box transcription factors — TCF-1, LEF-1, SOX-4 — and on
T-cell development. The people from this era are almost all in Table B, because PubMed did not
index their addresses: Mariëtte Oosterwegel, Marc Schilham, Petra Moerer, Jeroen Roose,
Vladimír Kořínek, Dennis Dooijes, Marc van Beest, Miranda Molenaar. The pivot came in 1997,
when Korinek and Barker showed that β-catenin/TCF-4 is constitutively active in APC-mutant
colon cancer — the paper that turned an immunology lab into a Wnt-and-cancer lab.

**2002–2015, the Hubrecht and Lgr5.** The lab moved to the Hubrecht Laboratory / NIOB and
found, in sequence, the Wnt/TCF intestinal signature (van der Flier), EphB-controlled cell
positioning (Batlle), Lgr5 as an adult stem-cell marker (Barker, 2007) and the intestinal
organoid culture (Sato, 2009). This is the period that produced the largest number of
independent group leaders per head.

**2015–present, organoids and pediatric oncology.** The group became a joint appointment with
the Princess Máxima Center. Work broadened to cancer organoid biobanks, CRISPR engineering in
organoids (Geurts, Andersson-Rolf), the microbiome (Puschhof, Pleguezuelos-Manzano), lung and
brain organoids (Dost; Artegiani and Hendriks), and pediatric tumour models. From 2022 Clevers
also heads Roche pRED and the Institute of Human Biology in Basel, and the most recent papers
carry Roche affiliations alongside the Utrecht ones.

### How far to trust this

The paper counts and first-co-publication years are exact — they are computed from the complete
PubMed record set and can be recomputed from `data/coauthors.csv`. The membership classification
is as good as PubMed's affiliation indexing allows, which means it is reliable from 2014 onward
and systematically incomplete before then. The role assignments were made one person at a time
from institutional pages, thesis-defence announcements and published biographies; they cover the
most prolific members and the alumni with a public research profile, not all 461 people. Roles
that could not be sourced to an explicit statement are marked "(inferred)", and people who
merely collaborated are marked as such rather than being left to look like lab members.
